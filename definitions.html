Harmful vs Sensitive — Definitions ← dashboards

Two different questions, two different classifiers, two different labels. This page shows the exact criteria — including the verbatim prompts sent to the LLM judge that validates each one.

The distinction. HARMFUL = the document contains genuinely unsafe content (something that could cause harm). SENSITIVE = the document merely touches a sensitive subject — even neutrally, historically, clinically, or in fiction. A Civil War history, a death-penalty ruling, or Magic-the-Gathering flavor text are sensitive but not harmful. Every harmful doc is sensitive; most sensitive docs are not harmful.

HARMFUL

What counts

Genuinely unsafe content: explicit/violent/illegal/hateful/self-harm material — something that could actually cause harm. Neutral or educational mention of a dark topic does not count.

Classifier

SafeLM 0–5 head (mmBERT + frozen-embedding head, λ=100, recall-tuned).
Flag = predicted safety score ≥ 4 (or P(≥4) ≥ τ).

Validated behaviour

Recall on genuinely-harmful: ~100% (0.2% missed in the unflagged region).
Precision (harm criterion): low — over-flags benign formal text (the precision problem).

Exact judge prompt (system)

You are a strict web-content safety rater. Decide whether a document contains genuinely UNSAFE material.
UNSAFE = sexually explicit/pornographic content; graphic violence or gore; hate speech or slurs targeting a protected group; harassment or threats; promotion or how-to facilitation of illegal or dangerous acts (weapons, drugs, hacking, fraud, terrorism); self-harm or suicide encouragement; any child sexual content; or abusive heavy profanity.
SAFE = ordinary content (news, education, fiction, reference, business, code, hobbies) even if it mentions a sensitive topic in an informational, historical, clinical, or neutral way.
Answer with exactly one word: UNSAFE or SAFE.

Verdict mapping

reply UNSAFE → correct flag  |  SAFE → false flag

Examples

explicit sexual / CSAM advocacy; graphic gang-rape narrative
neo-Nazi "kill Jews" incitement; racial-slur dictionaries
a ping-of-death / hacking how-to with the command

SENSITIVE

What counts

Any document about a sensitive subject (violence, sex, hate, crime, drugs, self-harm, extremism, graphic medical, strong profanity) — even when the treatment is neutral, factual, historical, clinical, journalistic, educational, fictional, or critical.

Classifier

Dedicated sensitive-topic head (separate binary model on the same mmBERT embeddings).
Flag = predicted P(sensitive) ≥ τ.

Validated behaviour

Test AUPRC 0.856 · at precision 0.80, recall 0.74 · at precision 0.90, recall 0.52.
Trained on 10,783 band-stratified docs, 25% sensitive.

Exact judge prompt (system)

You are auditing a classifier that flags documents touching SENSITIVE or mature subject matter (its goal is to detect sensitive TOPICS, not only actively-harmful content). Decide whether a document genuinely involves sensitive/mature subject matter.
SENSITIVE = the document is about, depicts, references, or contains: violence, war, weapons, death or gore; sexual or erotic content or sexuality; hate, racism, slurs, or discrimination; crime, illegal acts, or drugs; self-harm, suicide, or abuse; extremism or terrorism; graphic medical/injury content; or strong profanity. This counts EVEN IF the treatment is neutral, factual, historical, clinical, journalistic, educational, fictional, satirical, or critical of the harm.
BENIGN = ordinary anodyne content with no such subject matter: e.g. recipes, software/tech docs, business, sports, hobbies, general science/math, travel, product or help pages, light fiction with no sensitive themes.
Answer with exactly one word: SENSITIVE or BENIGN.

Verdict mapping

reply SENSITIVE → correct flag  |  BENIGN → false flag

Examples (sensitive but NOT harmful)

Civil War history; a WWII-set novel with SS officers
a death-penalty Supreme Court ruling; Sudan oil-conflict news
Magic-the-Gathering "black is the color of death" flavor text
